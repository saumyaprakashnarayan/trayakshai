What HPC Actually Is

High Performance Computing (HPC) is a cluster of computers working in parallel to solve larger problems faster. An HPC system includes high performance networking to keep all the computers talking to each other, and high performance data storage for all the data and outputs of the system.
HPC clusters pool processing nodes via low-latency fabrics like 400Gb/s InfiniBand. Workloads are parallelized via MPI (Message Passing Interface) and job schedulers (such as Slurm), executing concurrently across thousands of CPU and GPU cores with shared high-speed parallel file systems.
Dramatically accelerated simulation times, the ability to train massive deep learning models on petabyte datasets, scalable architecture that grows with organizational computational demand, and significant competitive advantage in research, genomics, and product engineering.
Thermal management and power density at rack scale, networking latency bottlenecks, storage I/O starvation of GPUs, software cluster management, and maintaining high sustained utilization across multi-tenant teams without operational friction.

What quantum computing Actually Is

Quantum computing is a revolutionary computational paradigm that leverages the laws of quantum mechanics—specifically superposition and entanglement—to perform calculations that are intractable for classical supercomputers, unlocking exponential leaps in problem-solving capability.
Unlike classical computers which encode information as binary bits (0 or 1), quantum systems use qubits. Due to superposition, qubits can represent both 0 and 1 at the same time. Through quantum entanglement and interference, algorithms manipulate these probability waves to isolate correct solutions with extreme speed.
Breakthrough capabilities across molecular chemistry, materials science simulation, battery chemistry modeling, cryptographic security, supply-chain portfolio optimization, and hybrid quantum-classical machine learning algorithms (such as variational quantum eigensolvers).
Quantum computing is still very much a research project, although commercially available solutions are very close. The biggest challenges currently are the extremely cold temperatures required to keep qubits stable (and then this uses a lot of electricity). While superposition and entanglement give quantum computing its power, they are also very unstable and lead to a lot of errors in calculations. Managing these errors is a field of study in and of itself.

What is HFT?

High Frequency Trading (HFT) is the computerised trading of any asset (shares, bonds, crypto). Traders use specialised programs to implement trading strategies with high speed servers, like the TrayakshAI eXtreme range.
Trading algorithms ingest direct market feeds through kernel-bypass network cards (such as Solarflare and Arista). Orders are processed in custom FPGA logic pipelines or single-core CPUs overclocked to 5.9GHz, executing orders into financial exchange matching engines in sub-microsecond intervals.
Deterministic tick-to-trade latency, dramatic reduction in market queue jitter, hardware-level FPGA parsing, and high-frequency closed-loop liquid cooling ensuring that all cores maintain sustained maximum clock rates 24/7 without thermal throttling.
Achieving peak core overclocking frequencies without silicon degradation; engineering custom sealed liquid loops resilient enough for co-location data centers; eliminating operating system interrupt jitter; and fine-tuning PCIe trace topologies for direct NIC-to-processor transmission.
